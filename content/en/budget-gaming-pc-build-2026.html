Why These Components?

CPU: AMD Ryzen 5 7600

The Ryzen 5 7600 is a 6-core / 12-thread processor based on the Zen 4 architecture. It delivers great gaming performance and supports DDR5, leaving room to upgrade to a 7800X3D later (same AM5 socket). Base clock is 3.8 GHz, boosting to 5.1 GHz. The included Wraith Stealth cooler is adequate for gaming loads.

GPU: AMD Radeon RX 7700 XT

The RX 7700 XT is the best GPU under $250. It ships with 12 GB GDDR6 — more than the RTX 4060 (8 GB) at a similar price. 12 GB matters in modern games with large texture packs. It also supports FSR 3 with Frame Generation for an extra performance boost.

Motherboard: MSI PRO B650M-A WiFi

A budget B650 board with WiFi 6E and DDR5 XMP/EXPO support. Sufficient for the Ryzen 5 7600 and future upgrades. The mATX form factor keeps case costs down.

RAM: Kingston Fury Beast DDR5-6000

32 GB DDR5-6000 hits the sweet spot for the AM5 platform. AMD recommends 6000 MHz as the "sweet spot" for Ryzen 7000 — maximum speed without overclocking risks. 32 GB covers any modern game plus background tasks.

SSD: Kingston NV3 1TB

PCIe 4.0 NVMe SSD with read speeds up to 6,000 MB/s. Games load fast and 1 TB is enough for starters. The B650 board has two M.2 slots for easy expansion later.

Conclusion

For $700–800 you can build a PC that handles any modern game at 1080p on max settings without compromise. The key rules: don't skimp on the GPU, make sure you have enough VRAM (12 GB minimum in 2026), and choose a platform with upgrade headroom. The AM5 platform will remain relevant for several more years — it's a solid investment.
