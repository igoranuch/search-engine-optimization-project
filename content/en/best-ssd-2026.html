Introduction

PCIe 4.0 and 5.0 NVMe SSDs are now standard in gaming PCs. But does a $175 premium SSD actually feel faster than a $55 budget drive in real use? In this comparison we test three of the most popular options: Samsung 990 Pro, WD Black SN850X, and Crucial T705 (PCIe 5.0). Spoiler: for gamers, the difference is smaller than you might expect.

Key Takeaway on Game Loading

The difference between the Samsung 990 Pro and the Crucial T705 (PCIe 5.0) is under 1 second when loading games. PCIe 5.0 gives a meaningful advantage only for large file operations (video editing, archive extraction). The budget Kingston NV3 trails by 3–4 seconds — noticeable but not critical for gaming.

Thermals & Throttling

The Crucial T705 without a heatsink reaches 85°C and begins thermal throttling — write speeds drop noticeably. If your case or motherboard doesn't include an M.2 heatsink, you'll need to buy one separately ($15–25). The Samsung 990 Pro and WD SN850X stay within safe thermal limits even without a heatsink.

Recommendations

Use Case Recommendation
Pure gaming PC Kingston NV3 or Samsung 990 Pro (difference is minimal)
Gaming + general use Samsung 990 Pro — stable thermals, great reliability
4K video editing / large files Crucial T705 (with heatsink) or WD SN850X
PlayStation 5 upgrade WD SN850X — officially recommended by Sony
Tightest budget Kingston NV3 1TB (~$55) — more than enough for gaming

Conclusion

Paying $175 for the Crucial T705 over the $140 Samsung 990 Pro makes no sense for gaming — the $35 premium doesn't translate to a meaningful difference in game load times. The Samsung 990 Pro is the best all-around pick thanks to reliable thermals, consistent performance, and wide availability. If you're on a tight budget, any PCIe 4.0 NVMe drive (Kingston NV3, Crucial P3 Plus) for $50–60 will fully satisfy your gaming needs.
