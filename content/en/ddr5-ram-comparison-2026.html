Introduction

With the shift to AM5 (AMD) and LGA1700/1851 (Intel), DDR5 is now the standard. But the market offers kits ranging from DDR5-4800 to DDR5-8000+. Is it worth paying a premium for higher frequencies? We tested five popular kits and measured their real-world impact on FPS.

Test Setup

Tests were run on: AMD Ryzen 7 7800X3D, MSI MEG X670E ACE, RTX 4070, Windows 11 23H2. All kits installed with EXPO (AMD's XMP equivalent) enabled.

Frequency vs. Timings — What Matters More?

Compare the Corsair DDR5-6000 CL30 against the Kingston DDR5-6000 CL36 — at the same frequency, lower timings give +4–6% FPS. The G.Skill DDR5-7200 CL34 vs. Kingston DDR5-6000 CL36 delivers +8–10% FPS, but costs more than twice as much.

The rule is simple: DDR5-6000 CL30 or better is the sweet spot for AM5. Everything beyond that is diminishing returns — each additional 10% of frequency yields progressively smaller FPS gains.

Is DDR5-7600 Worth the Money?

The Corsair Dominator Titanium DDR5-7600 ($185) delivers roughly +10% average FPS over the Kingston DDR5-6000 ($65). For $120 more, that's not an efficient upgrade. That money is better spent on a faster GPU or a larger SSD.

Conclusion

For a gaming PC, DDR5-6000 with CL30–32 timings is the ideal price-to- performance sweet spot. This is AMD's recommended frequency for Ryzen 7000, requires no complex BIOS tuning, and delivers near-maximum gaming performance. Want a bit more? DDR5-6400 CL32 adds a few percent at a reasonable premium. DDR5-7200 and above is for benchmark chasers, not everyday gamers.
